# AI Tooling: It Might Change Your Tuesday

**Audience:** Consultants / generalists (Netlight colleagues, varying technical depth)
**Duration:** ~20 minutes
**Tone:** Grounded, slightly provocative, practical — cuts through hype without being cynical

## Opening Hook
Open with the noise: "AI will change everything." "The future is here." You've heard it. It's exhausting. Then flip it: *"Here's my honest experience — AI didn't change my life. But it did change my Tuesday."* Name something specific and relatable — writing a meeting summary, drafting a ticket from a Slack thread, documenting something nobody wanted to document. Small. Real. That's the entry point.

## Context: The Real Problem with the Hype
The hype isn't wrong about AI's capability — LLMs genuinely can do impressive, complex things. The problem is the gap between "AI will change everything" and "what do I actually do on Monday?" That gap is what stops people from starting. We see the ceiling and have no idea where the ladder is. Set up the honest picture: the capability is real, but the entry point is the hard part — and that's what this talk is about.

## Section 1: The Entry Point — Tedious, Not Difficult
The best way in isn't to tackle the most impressive thing AI can do. It's to find the thing in your week that's tedious but not hard. Documentation, tickets, meeting notes, status updates, boilerplate, research summaries. These aren't intellectually demanding — they just consume time and drain energy. Starting here isn't settling. It's strategy. This is where you build the intuition, the trust, and the workflow that eventually lets you do harder things.

## Section 2: The Tooling Landscape — So Much Stuff
There are endless tools: Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, custom GPTs, MCP servers, sub-agents, memory systems, skills, CLI workflows... it's overwhelming. Surface the real question the audience is probably asking: *"What do I actually use?"* Acknowledge honestly: there's no universal answer. What matters is that each of these tools is solving a slightly different problem — and the mistake is trying to adopt everything at once.

## Section 3: What I Use — A Personal Take
Get specific, but frame it as personal testimony: *"I can only tell you what worked for me."* Show what these tools look like in practice — skills (reusable prompt workflows), sub-agents (parallel tasks, protected context), memory (persistent context across sessions), MCP servers (connecting AI to your actual tools). Be honest about failure: the first few attempts didn't stick. Tried Obsidian with MCP — the tools were there but the workflow wasn't right yet. Iterated, wrapped the MCP tools in skills, shaped it to how I actually work. Now I use it every day. That's the arc: not adoption, but iteration toward fit.

## Turning Point: Find One Thing and Make It Yours
The insight: stop optimizing for the perfect setup. The consultants who get value from AI tooling aren't the ones with the most sophisticated stack — they're the ones who picked *one workflow*, made it theirs, and repeated it until it was effortless. Your Tuesday changes not when you adopt AI, but when one specific thing becomes automatic. That's the shift. And it's also the beginning of something bigger.

## Section: The Ceiling Is Higher Than You Think
Once one thing clicks, you understand the tool better. You trust it more. You give it harder problems. This is the progression nobody talks about: it's not that LLMs suddenly become capable of complex work — it's that *you* become capable of directing them toward it. Large refactors, multi-step research, autonomous workflows, architectural decisions — these are real things people are doing with AI today. They didn't start there. They started with a meeting summary. The ceiling is real. You reach it by building up, not by starting there.

## Closing
Land on the concrete ask: pick one thing from your last week that was tedious-but-not-difficult. Try to offload it this week — even partially. It doesn't have to be perfect. It just has to save you 20 minutes and teach you something. If it does, you'll be curious about the next thing. That curiosity compounds. *That's* how it changes your work — not in a flash, but one Tuesday at a time. The ceiling was always there. Now you have a ladder.

**Key takeaways:**
1. The capability is real — the gap is knowing where to start
2. Start with tedious-not-difficult: it's strategy, not settling
3. One workflow, made yours, repeated — that's the ladder to the harder stuff
