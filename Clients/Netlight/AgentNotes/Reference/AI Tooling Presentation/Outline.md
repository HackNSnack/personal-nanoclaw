# AI Tooling: It Might Change Your Tuesday — Slide Outline

**Slides:** 15
**Estimated duration:** ~20 minutes

---

### 01 — Title
- **Type:** title
- **Purpose:** Set the tone — grounded, personal, not another hype talk
- **Key message:** "It might change your Tuesday" — a deliberately modest but intriguing promise
- **Content:** Title: "It might change your Tuesday" / Subtitle: "An honest take on AI tooling"
- **Speaker notes:** Let it land quietly. Don't explain it yet.

---

### 02 — The Noise
- **Type:** quote
- **Purpose:** Open with what the audience already knows and is tired of
- **Key message:** The hype is loud, familiar, and exhausting
- **Content:** Hype quotes in different sizes, overlapping slightly, filling the slide — visual chaos by design. Attribute a few to recognisable sources (LinkedIn, tech blogs, conference keynotes). "AI will change everything." "The future is now." "10x engineer." "Agents will replace developers."
- **Speaker notes:** Say nothing for a beat. Let the audience read it and recognise themselves in the fatigue.

---

### 03 — The Honest Flip
- **Type:** comparison
- **Purpose:** Reframe immediately — personal, grounded, real
- **Key message:** "AI didn't change my life. But it did change my Tuesday."
- **Content:** Two-column comparison. Left: "What they promised" — 2-3 hype phrases. Right: "What actually changed" — a specific, mundane Tuesday example (a meeting summary that took 30 seconds instead of 20 minutes). The big line as caption underneath: *"AI didn't change my life. But it did change my Tuesday."*
- **Speaker notes:** Pause after the caption line. Let it breathe. This is the anchor for the whole talk.

---

### 04 — But the Capability Is Real
- **Type:** card-grid
- **Purpose:** Establish that the ceiling is genuinely high — this isn't a cynical talk
- **Key message:** LLMs can do impressive, complex things — that part of the hype is true
- **Content:** 4-6 cards, each with a concrete specific example. E.g. "Migrating a legacy service, file by file, with context held across the whole session." "Turning 12 browser tabs into a structured briefing doc." "Generating a full test suite from a spec, then fixing its own failures." Not abstract categories — specific, credible things.
- **Speaker notes:** Say this with conviction. You're not here to deflate — you're here to redirect.

---

### 05 — The Real Problem
- **Type:** diagram
- **Purpose:** Identify the actual gap — between capability and entry point
- **Key message:** The problem isn't the ceiling — it's that nobody tells you where the ladder is
- **Content:** Two panels. Left: "The ceiling" — label it with the impressive capabilities from slide 04. Right: "Monday morning" — a blank prompt, a calendar, a question mark. A visible gap between the two panels, labelled: *"Where's the ladder?"*
- **Speaker notes:** This is the question the whole talk answers. Name it clearly and let the diagram do the work.

---

### 06 — The Entry Point: Tedious, Not Difficult
- **Type:** comparison
- **Purpose:** Introduce the core starting strategy — and reframe it as strategy, not settling
- **Key message:** Start with what's tedious but not hard — that's the ladder, not the ceiling
- **Content:** Two-column comparison. Left: "Wrong question" — *"Will AI replace me?"* / *"What's AI best at?"* Right: "Better question" — *"What do I do that costs time but not brainpower?"* Below both columns: 4-5 example task categories as small tags/chips: Documentation, Tickets, Meeting notes, Boilerplate, Status updates.
- **Speaker notes:** Emphasise: this isn't the ceiling. This is the ladder. Starting here isn't settling — it's strategy.

---

### 07 — What That Looks Like
- **Type:** card-grid
- **Purpose:** Make the entry point concrete with recognisable examples
- **Key message:** You already have a list — you just haven't thought of it that way
- **Content:** 6 cards, each with a task name and a one-line concrete description. E.g. "Meeting notes — paste the transcript, get a structured summary with decisions and actions." "Ticket from conversation — paste the Slack thread, get a drafted Jira ticket." "Status update — describe what you did, get a formatted update ready to send."
- **Speaker notes:** Ask them to mentally pick one. Plant the seed for the CTA at the end.

---

### 08 — The Landscape
- **Type:** diagram
- **Purpose:** Surface the overwhelm honestly — then give it structure
- **Key message:** There are too many tools and no universal answer — but they're not all the same thing
- **Content:** Visual map grouped by category. Four clusters: *IDE & code tools* (Cursor, Copilot), *CLI & agent tools* (Claude Code), *Workflow & automation* (skills, sub-agents, memory), *Integrations* (MCP servers, custom GPTs). Header or subtitle: *"So... what do you actually use?"*
- **Speaker notes:** Don't dismiss any of them. The grouping helps but the overwhelm is still real — that's intentional.

---

### 09 — It Doesn't Matter Which One
- **Type:** comparison
- **Purpose:** Resolve the overwhelm — remove the pressure of choosing perfectly
- **Key message:** Finding your fit matters more than finding the "best" tool
- **Content:** Two-column visual contrast. Left: person with 10 tools, none mastered, arrows going everywhere — labelled "busy, not productive." Right: person with one workflow, repeated, deepened over time — labelled "this is where the value is." Caption: *"There's no one-fits-all. The mistake is trying to adopt everything at once."*
- **Speaker notes:** This should feel like a relief. Land it that way.

---

### 10 — The Tools in Practice
- **Type:** card-grid
- **Purpose:** Show concretely what a personal stack looks like — framed as personal testimony
- **Key message:** These tools each solve something specific — together they form a workflow
- **Content:** Subtitle: *"I can only tell you what worked for me."* 2×2 card grid. Each card: tool name (large), what it does (one line), concrete personal example (one line, smaller). E.g. *Skills — reusable prompt workflows — "I have a skill that turns any meeting note into a structured Obsidian entry."* Sub-agents, Memory, MCP servers each get the same treatment.
- **Speaker notes:** Stay shallow. Show the shape, not the depth. Four cards = four minutes of content if you let it breathe.

---

### 11 — The Failure Story
- **Type:** content
- **Purpose:** Acknowledge friction — iteration is the method, not instant adoption
- **Key message:** The first attempt probably won't stick, and that's fine
- **Content:** Visual timeline: Step 1: "Set up Obsidian + MCP" → Step 2: "Didn't stick — tools were there, workflow wasn't" → Step 3: "Wrapped MCP in a skill" → Step 4: "Use it every day." Optionally show a simplified mock of what the skill invocation looks like — a single line command.
- **Speaker notes:** Tell it as a story. It's proof of the process, not an apology.

---

### 12 — The Turning Point
- **Type:** comparison
- **Purpose:** Deliver the core insight — and connect it forward to the bigger picture
- **Key message:** One workflow, made yours, repeated — that's how you start and how you scale
- **Content:** Two side-by-side profiles. Left: "Most sophisticated stack" — complex diagram, many tools, no clear outcome. Right: "One workflow, made theirs" — one tool, one use case, repeated, deepened. Arrow pointing right labelled "that's where the value is." Headline: *"The people getting value aren't the ones with the most tools."*
- **Speaker notes:** Slow down here. Then add: "And it's also just the beginning."

---

### 13 — The Ceiling Is Higher Than You Think
- **Type:** diagram
- **Purpose:** Show the destination — where the ladder actually leads
- **Key message:** The complex, impressive things are real — and this is the path to them
- **Content:** A ladder diagram, rungs labelled bottom to top. Bottom rung (labelled *"Where you start"*): Meeting summaries, ticket drafts, status updates. Middle: Documentation, boilerplate generation, research digests. Upper middle: Multi-file refactors, structured research synthesis. Top (labelled *"Where it can go"*): Autonomous multi-step workflows, architectural reasoning. Caption: *"The people at the top started at the bottom."*
- **Speaker notes:** Don't oversell. Let the diagram speak. "This is where it leads" is more credible than "this is what's possible."

---

### 14 — The Ask
- **Type:** content
- **Purpose:** Deliver the CTA — low bar, specific, forward-looking
- **Key message:** Pick one thing this week — it's not just about saving time, it's the first rung
- **Content:** A prompt the audience can mentally fill in: *"Think of your last week. What took time, but not brainpower?"* Below: three-step action, numbered and clean. 1) Pick one thing. 2) Try to offload it this week. 3) See what you learn.
- **Speaker notes:** Make the bar feel like almost nothing. The point is to start, not to impress.

---

### 15 — Closing
- **Type:** closing
- **Purpose:** Close the loop — echo the opening, land the new meaning
- **Key message:** The ceiling was always there. Now you have a ladder.
- **Content:** The ladder diagram from slide 13, zoomed out and smaller. Two lines over it: *"The ceiling was always there. Now you have a ladder."* Then the title line underneath: *"It might change your Tuesday."*
- **Speaker notes:** Don't add anything new. The opening line lands differently now. Let it.

---

**Key takeaways:**
1. The capability is real — the gap is knowing where to start
2. Start with tedious-not-difficult: it's strategy, not settling
3. One workflow, made yours, repeated — that's the ladder to the harder stuff
