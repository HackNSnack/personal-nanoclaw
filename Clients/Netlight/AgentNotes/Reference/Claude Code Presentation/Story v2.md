# AI Tooling — What Actually Works

**Audience:** Mixed technical (developers, tech leads, managers)
**Duration:** ~30 minutes
**Tone:** Grounded, practical, honest — no hype

## Opening Hook
Start with the noise. The headlines, the Twitter threads, the endless demos of AI writing entire apps in seconds. Ask the audience: how much of that has actually changed your Monday morning? Probably not much. This talk isn't about the future. It's about what you can do differently next week.

## The Honest Picture
Strip the hype away and something simpler emerges: AI doesn't solve everything, won't replace you, and won't 10x your output overnight. But it *can* reduce real friction in specific, predictable places — if you know where to point it. The gap between "I've heard of it" and "this is part of how I work" is real, and it's not about adopting a new philosophy. It's about finding one useful thing and starting there.

## What Actually Helps — By Task
Forget tool categories. Organize around what you're actually trying to do. Exploring a codebase you've never seen before. Writing the first draft of a test suite. Refactoring repetitive code. Generating boilerplate you'd otherwise copy-paste. For each of these: what does it look like without AI, and what does it look like with it? Concrete, relatable, Tuesday-afternoon tasks — not moonshots.

## The Cost of Not Trying
Most people are watching from the sidelines — waiting for the right moment, the right tool, a less chaotic week. But here's the thing: the gap isn't between people who use AI and people who don't. It's between people who use it *deliberately* and those who haven't started. Every week you skip is a week you're not building the intuition for where it helps and where it doesn't. The tools keep improving. The gap keeps growing.

## Turning Point
The shift isn't about AI doing your job. It's about what you choose to spend your attention on. When the tedious parts get faster, you spend more time on the hard, interesting problems — the ones that actually need a human. That's not hype. That's just a better workday.

## Closing
Don't try to change everything. Pick one task from this week — something repetitive, something tedious, something you wished was already done. Try it with AI next week. See if it helps. If it does, try another. That's the whole plan.

---

**Key takeaways:**
1. AI reduces real friction — but only if you know where to point it
2. Deliberate, task-focused use beats passive skepticism
3. Start with one thing this week and judge for yourself
